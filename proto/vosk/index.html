2 · Push-to-talk: 36 phrases

Tap the mic, say the phrase, then stop talking. It stops on its own after 1.2 s of silence. A match moves on by itself.

3 · Hands-free: 14 turns, then at least 15 minutes

The mic stays open with no button. Prop the phone up by the board, screen on, and step back to the oche (about 2.4 m). Say each turn as prompted: some in one breath, some one dart at a time with pauses, plus a checkout on dart 1 and on dart 2, a bust, and "next"/"done" early. Phrases that don't parse are ignored and logged. A turn the app isn't sure of (a dart marked ?) waits for you to say “yes” or “no”, or to tap Confirm / Discard, and you hear a two-note tone when that happens. Then leave it running for at least 15 minutes (30 if you can) for the battery and endurance test.

iPhone Safari can't read the battery level, so type it from Settings › Battery (or the status bar).

“Test speech output” is how the app will work: the mic is muted while it speaks. The “mic left open” version checks whether the phone's echo cancellation alone would stop the recogniser from hearing its own voice.

How did “One hundred and eighty” sound?

Background test: for 5 minutes, talk normally with someone and play music (or the TV) without calling any darts. Everything the app would have acted on counts as a false trigger, with and without the wake word.
